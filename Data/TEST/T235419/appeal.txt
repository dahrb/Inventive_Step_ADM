The applicant filed an appeal against the Examining Division's decision to refuse European patent application No. 16196899.5. II. The Examining Division held that claims 1 and 6 of the main request then on file did not meet the requirements of Article 123(2) EPC and that the subject-matter of auxiliary requests 1 and 2 did not involve an inventive step starting from D2 = US 2003/0036674 in combination with D1 = US 2005/0177061. III. The Board summoned the appellant to oral proceedings. In a communication it conveyed its preliminary opinion that claims 1, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8 of the main request and auxiliary request I, and claims 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 of auxiliary request II, all filed with the statement of grounds of appeal, did not meet the requirements of Article 76(1) EPC. It was further stated in the communication that an amended request overcoming these objections could, in its opinion, meet the requirement of inventive step under Article 56 EPC. IV. On 13 October 2023, the appellant filed an amended main request addressing the objections raised. The appellant requested as a main request that the decision under appeal be set aside and that a patent be granted on the basis of the claims according to the amended main request. V. The Board cancelled the oral proceedings. VI. Claim 1 of the main request filed on 13 October 2023 reads as follows. "A method for measuring a capacitance for use in detecting the development of a pressure ulcer at a target location of a patient's skin, comprising: positioning a flexible substrate (16) adjacent the target location of the patient's skin, wherein on the flexible substrate (16) are embedded an array (14) of bipolar radiofrequency (RF) sensors (24, 26), wherein each sensor comprises a first electrode (36) and a second electrode (38) and wherein each sensor (24, 26) is individually coupled to an interface electronic package (22), wherein the electronics package (22) is configured for independent interrogation and excitation of each of the bipolar RF sensors (24, 26), and data retrieval from each of the bipolar RF sensors (24, 26); conforming the flexible substrate (16) to the patient's skin at the target location; exciting the bipolar RF sensors (24, 26) to emit RF energy into the patient's skin derma; and measuring a capacitance at the target location of the patient's skin as an indicator of the Sub-Epidermal Moisture (SEM) Claim 6 of the main request reads as follows. "An apparatus for measuring a capacitance for use in detecting the development of a pressure ulcer at a target location of a patient's skin, the apparatus comprising: a flexible substrate (16) wherein on the flexible substrate is embedded an array (14) of bipolar radiofrequency (RF) sensors (24, 26) wherein each sensor comprises a first electrode (36) and a second electrode (38); and an interface electronics package (22), wherein each sensor (24, 26) comprising a first electrode (36) and a second electrode (38) is individually coupled to the interface electronics package, wherein the interface electronics (22) are configured to control the bipolar RF sensors (24, 26) for emitting and receiving RF energy to interrogate the patient's skin, so as to measure a capacitance at the target location of the patient's skin as an indicator of the Sub-Epidermal Moisture (SEM) at the target location, and wherein the interface electronics (22) are configured for independent interrogation and excitation of each of the bipolar RF sensors (24, 26), and data retrieval from each of the bipolar RF sensors (24, 26)." VII. The appellant's arguments relevant to the present decision can be summarised as follows. Main request - added subject-matter In claim 1, the feature that "on the flexible substrate (16) are embedded an array (14) of electrodes comprising first electrodes (36) and second electrodes (38) forming bipolar radiofrequency (RF) sensors (24, 26)" had been replaced by "on the flexible substrate (16) are embedded an array (14) of bipolar radio-frequency (RF) sensors (24, 26), wherein each sensor comprises a first electrode (36) and a second electrode (38)". The amendment was based on the disclosure of paragraphs [0097], [0040], [0079], [0087] and [0092] of the parent application as filed and the same paragraphs of the present application as filed. Consequently, in claim 1, the feature "wherein the first electrodes (36) and second electrodes (38) are each individually coupled to an interface electronic package (22)" had been replaced by "wherein each sensor (24, 26) is individually coupled to an interface electronics package (22)". The amendment was based on the disclosure of paragraphs [0097], [0041], [0045] and [0076] of the parent application as filed and the same paragraphs of the present application as filed. Claim 6 had been amended correspondingly. Claim 3 had been amended to replace the feature "wherein the plurality of bipolar RF sensors (24, 26) that are spaced across the flexible substrate (16)" with "wherein the array of bipolar RF sensors (24, 26) is spaced across the flexible substrate (16)". Claim 4 had consequently been amended to refer to an "array of bipolar RF sensors" rather than a "plurality of bipolar RF sensors". Support for the amendment could be found in paragraph [0035] of both the parent application as filed and the present application as filed. Claim 7 had been amended to replace the feature "wherein each of the electrodes (36, 38) is individually coupled to the interface electronics (22) to form a plurality of RF sensors (24, 26) that independently interrogate the skin" with "wherein each of the bipolar RF sensors (24, 26) is individually coupled to the interface electronics package (22) to allow for independent interrogation of the skin". Claim 8 had consequently been amended to refer to an "array of bipolar RF sensors" rather than a "plurality of bipolar RF sensors". Support for the amendment could be found in paragraph [0045] of the parent application as filed as well as of the present application as filed. Hence, claims 1, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8 of the main request as amended met the requirements of Articles 76(1) and 123(2) EPC. Main request - inventive step The person skilled in the art would not combine D2 with D1 due to the structural differences between the sensors in D2 and D1. The general idea of the antenna system configuration disclosed in D2 differed substantially from that of the invention in the present case. D2 did not disclose bipolar sensors but a dual-patch antenna configuration. The antennas could be arranged in two linear arrays (Figure 9B). As shown in Figures 7A and 7B, each sensor element comprised a substrate 430, on which an antenna 420 was positioned. A superstrate layer 410, which contacted the skin, covered the antenna 420. As mentioned in paragraph [0116], each of the patch antennas of the dual patch worked independently as a one-electrode measurement system and therefore did not form bipolar sensors. D1 described an apparatus with an electromagnetic probe 24, which included an inner electrode, an outer grounded electrode 32, a Teflon insulator 31 between the electrodes, a coaxial cable, and an electronic unit 34 (Figures 1 and 2, paragraphs [0024] and [0025]). The probe 24 was simply an open-ended coaxial cable (D1, paragraph [0012]), which was separate from the electronic unit 34. The Teflon insulator 31 was sandwiched between the inner electrode 30 and the outer electrode 32, i.e. the electrodes were not embedded in a flexible structure. The sensor structure of D1 was based on a single electrode that both emitted a signal and received a reflection of that signal. Furthermore, the device in D1 required conductive contact between the sensor and the skin to measure phase differences that correlated with the water content in the skin. This would not be possible if the sensor in D1 were covered by an insulation layer. Moreover, the requirement for conductive contact between the sensor and the skin precluded the multiplying of the sensors in D1, e.g. by arranging a plurality of probes in a sensor array, whereby each sensor would be in conductive contact with the skin. Hence, the person skilled in the art would not replace the antenna configuration in D2 with a plurality of sensors as disclosed in D1. Consequently, the subject-matter of claims 1 and 6 of the main request involved an inventive step in view of a combination of D2 with D1.
